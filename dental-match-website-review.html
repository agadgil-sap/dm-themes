Website direction review · 2 October 2026

One identity.
Three ways to begin.

Dental Match helps people exploring implants take a manageable first step. These directions change how that first step feels, while keeping the agreed brand and complete patient journey.

The agreed foundation

L22 + Friendly Clarity

The smile wordmark, plum, lilac and warm gold. Manrope gives the identity its character; DM Sans keeps the information and forms easy to read.

Design recommendation

Lead with clear & welcoming.

It best reflects the brief’s anxiety, cost and delayed-care barriers. Its concern selector offers a useful response before asking for details. This is a design judgement, not a measured conversion result. The guided version is a useful alternative to test with patients who already want to act.

Variation 1 Recommended starting point

Clear & welcoming

Reassurance through practical, useful interaction.

Its strongest choice

“What is on your mind?” makes anxiety, cost and life pressures part of the conversation. Rounded lilac components feel closest to Friendly Clarity.

Its tradeoff

The reassurance panel adds a step before the main enquiry. On mobile it follows the headline, so the primary invitation remains available first.

What to test next

Do patients recognise their concern, feel understood and continue? Ask them to explain what the service does in their own words.

Variation 2

Your guided first step

A stronger invitation to start the matching journey.

Its strongest choice

The hero is the first useful question. A selected starting point carries into the enquiry, with a chance to add concerns or change the choice.

Its tradeoff

The large plum panel and early question are more directive. Someone who needs reassurance first may prefer the welcoming approach.

What to test next

Can people choose confidently, including “I’m not sure yet”? Compare completed enquiries and where patients pause once a live service is ready.

Explore guided ↗
Variation 3

Space to decide

A quieter, question-led introduction to implants.

Its strongest choice

White space, lighter type and open questions give patients room to read. It suits people who want to understand the process before speaking.

Its tradeoff

It has less of B’s overt friendliness. The reading panel introduces more deliberation, and some answers stay hidden until opened.

What to test next

Does the quieter presentation build understanding or defer action? Check which answers patients look for before they begin.

Explore considered ↗

A second pass on the details.

The visual audit covered inner pages, cards, callouts, error states, review and confirmation at 1440px, 820px, 390px and 320px.

Spacing and readability corrected

  • The considered theme’s white cards had no side padding. They now inherit the shared card insets, with bottom-aligned links.
  • Shared padding rules for panels and cards replace conflicting breakpoint overrides.
  • The guided form’s doubled 50px button gap is now 24px.
  • The FAQ help box has 32px of separation on desktop and 24px on phones.
  • Standalone callouts no longer add a redundant bottom margin.
  • Concern responses size to their content, without a large empty area.
  • Mobile body text and choices are larger; primary form actions use the full available width.
  • Review Edit actions align with their label and value, without an extra empty grid row.
  • Footer links, edit actions and preview toggles have larger touch targets.
  • The fallback page’s two buttons now have a proper gap.

A consistent inset

The complete enquiry journey.

Shared components keep behaviour consistent as you switch between the three directions.

  1. 1

    A starting point

    Choose an implant interest or uncertainty. Add optional concerns about anxiety, cost, life pressures or delayed care.

  2. 2

    Your practical needs

    Postcode, travel and timing. Leading-zero postcodes work, and exploring is a valid answer.

  3. 3

    Contact preference

    Use sample details. Choose phone or email; only the preferred contact method is required.

  4. 4

    Review & confirmation

    Edit an earlier answer, acknowledge the demo, then see an honest confirmation. No enquiry is sent.

Interactive demo only. Answers stay in page memory, survive a style switch and clear on completion or reload. The question form also has validation and its own confirmation.

Verification evidence

Reviewed as a working website.

  • All three patient journeysReal-browser checks cover navigation, validation, review editing, style switching, matching and question-form confirmation.
  • Mobile widths: 320px and 390pxAll pages and matching steps were checked for horizontal overflow. Menu opening and Escape focus behaviour were exercised.
  • Keyboard and historyThe skip link preserves the current page. Browser back restores the route and style together. Error summaries receive focus.
  • Nine focused validation checksPostcodes, phone and email preferences, explicit demo acknowledgement, fresh form state and escaping of user-entered text.

Critique applied during the build

  • Compacted the guided hero from four tall rows to a two-by-two choice grid.
  • Stopped an earlier hero answer overriding a later treatment link.
  • Included the optional concern step after the guided first question.
  • Reset completed enquiries to the first step with cleared contact details.
  • Removed blame from wording about care that has had to wait.
  • Corrected skip-link routing, mobile Escape handling and variation state during browser back.

Before this becomes a live lead service.

Confirm the participating clinic network, who contacts a patient first, and the matching criteria. Connect a lead destination, define the consent and privacy wording for actual data collection, and verify the contact and delivery process end to end.

Then test these directions with patients exploring implants. Measure understanding and comfort alongside enquiry completion. Real clinic information and approved photography can add human context once those assets are available.

The implant overview references healthdirect’s dental implant information. Clinical suitability, treatment choices and costs remain questions for a dentist and the participating clinic.
